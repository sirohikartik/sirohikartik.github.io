DICE-RoBERTa: Dense Image Captioning with Entropy and RoBERTa for Efficient Video Classification

Nitesh Bharot, Kartik Sirohi, Nisha Chaurasia, Priyanka Verma, John G. Breslin
Insight Research Ireland Centre for Data Analytics (University of Galway) & Dr. B.R. Ambedkar NIT Jalandhar

Motivation

State-of-the-art video action recognition models achieve high accuracy on benchmarks like UCF101 (>97%), but they require clusters of high-end GPUs (e.g., multiple H100s) and long training times (20–50 hours). More importantly, these models operate as black boxes: when a model misclassifies an action (such as confusing bodyweight squats with lunges), practitioners cannot inspect why the failure occurred. Existing explainable methods rely on post-hoc saliency heatmaps or dense numerical concept matrices, which decouple visual context from chronological action.

DICE-RoBERTa addresses these limitations through three principles: accessibility (training on free-tier GPUs), interpretability (using natural language as an intermediate bottleneck), and efficiency (minimizing inference GFLOPs).

The DICE-RoBERTa Framework

DICE-RoBERTa decouples visual perception from temporal reasoning through a three-stage pipeline:

  • Stage 1: Histogram-Based Entropy Filtering. Before passing frames to any neural network, we compute the Shannon entropy difference of pixel intensities between consecutive frames ($\Delta H = |H(f_t) - H(f_{prev})|$). Frames with $\Delta H < \tau_{hist} = 0.01$ are visually redundant and rejected, reducing the number of processed frames by roughly 65%.
  • Stage 2: Visual-to-Linguistic Encoding. Selected frames are processed by Florence-2-Base (0.23B parameters) fine-tuned with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA, rank $r=64$, $\alpha=128$). Florence-2 generates dense, semantic captions describing the actors, objects, and interactions in the frame (e.g., "holding violin", "bowing strings").
  • Stage 3: Linguistic Classification. Frame captions are aggregated chronologically into a single text document and classified using fine-tuned RoBERTa-Large (355M parameters) to predict the final action label among 101 classes.

What Was Found

  • Competitive accuracy with intrinsic explainability: DICE-RoBERTa achieves 85.0% top-1 accuracy on UCF101, significantly outperforming comparable self-supervised baselines (C3D at 58.8%, Clip Ordering at 64.9%).
  • 41% lower inference cost than MoCLIP-Lite: By discarding ~65% of frames via histogram entropy filtering, the pipeline requires only 9.8 GFLOPs per video (vs. 16.9 GFLOPs for MoCLIP-Lite and 64.1 GFLOPs for ResNet3D-50).
  • Trained on free-tier hardware: The complete pipeline was trained in 6 hours and 48 minutes on a single free NVIDIA Tesla P100 (16GB) on Kaggle (Stage 1: 4h13m, Stage 2: 1h28m, Stage 3: 1h06m), democratizing research without requiring expensive multi-GPU infrastructure.
  • Transparent error diagnosis: Because decisions flow through natural language captions, failure modes are directly inspectable. The model excels on object-centric actions (14 classes with 1.00 F1), but struggles on subtle bodyweight motions (such as BodyWeightSquats, F1: 0.33). Examining the text logs reveals that the vision captioner produced generic descriptions like "person exercising indoors" rather than specific kinematic cues like "person squatting with bent knees"—pinpointing the exact module to improve.
